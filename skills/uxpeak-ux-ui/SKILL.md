# UXpeak UX/UI

Produce interfaces that help people complete their tasks, with clear decisions, honest information and testable behavior. Treat visual polish as support for those outcomes.

This independent skill synthesizes UXpeak's public channel; it is not endorsed by UXpeak. The source register covers 23 public items inventoried on 2026-10-31: 19 full caption transcripts reviewed, three description-only reviews and one Short visually sampled. Do not describe this as watching or fully reviewing every video. See [sources](references/sources.md) for coverage and limits.

## Select the relevant references

- **Evaluate:** read [audit](references/audit.md). Inspect the actual interface and relevant states when available; distinguish observations from assumptions.
- **Design or redesign:** read [patterns](references/patterns.md) and the brief/design portions of [delivery](references/delivery.md).
- **Implement:** read [delivery](references/delivery.md), with the relevant patterns. Build usable interactions in the user's stack and verify the rendered result.
- **Figma, animation or interaction prototype:** read [prototyping](references/prototyping.md). A polished prototype is not proof of production usability.
- **Explain attribution or update the skill:** read [sources](references/sources.md). Source IDs in references point to that register. Load only the relevant references, not the entire research history.

## Establish the decision context

Identify the user, primary task, intended outcome, frequency, entry point, consequences of mistakes, platform and input methods. Respect existing brand, technical stack, design system and user constraints. Inspect supplied artifacts before asking questions. Proceed with explicit assumptions for reversible choices; ask only when missing information materially changes the solution.

For each meaningful recommendation connect **user need → observed friction → proposed change → tradeoff → verification**. Do not remove useful information merely to make a screen cleaner. Do not add elaborate controls merely to make it look more advanced. Prefer the simplest control that fits frequency, precision and available space.

## Apply the critical filter

- Treat channel examples and claimed conversion improvements as hypotheses, not verified evidence for this user's product. Do not invent user research, test results or uplift predictions.
- Use editable, reversible defaults when they help. Never silently opt people into subscriptions, fees or consequential consent.
- Make prices, recurring charges, cancellation conditions and estimate uncertainty clear before commitment. Do not manufacture precise prices from uncertain estimates.
- Use only real reviews, stock levels, activity counts, deadlines and progress. Reject false urgency, threats of loss, confirmshaming and misleading comparisons between recurring charges and one-time prices.
- Preserve necessary labels, visible state and recovery options. Do not infer ability from age or use demographics as a substitute for research.
- Use motion and 3D when they explain state or serve a justified brand goal. Provide reduced-motion and usable static behavior; assess load and interaction costs.
- Do not turn incidental health, financial or product claims in a design tutorial into domain advice.
- Use original or appropriately licensed assets. Borrow principles and interaction ideas without copying proprietary designs or paid course materials.

## Deliver and verify

Match fidelity and output to the request: a focused audit, a flow and screen specification, an interactive prototype or working application. Include relevant empty, loading, error, success and recovery states. Reuse coherent components and tokens rather than styling each screen independently.

For implementation, exercise the primary task and a meaningful failure path, inspect representative narrow and wide layouts, and check applicable keyboard/touch behavior. Record what actually ran and what remains unverified. Compilation, a screenshot or a visual resemblance alone does not establish usability or accessibility.

Return the usable artifact first, with the key design decisions, verification evidence and material limitations. Do not burden a small change with a full audit ceremony.
